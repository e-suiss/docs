## Principles of the model

- **Nine concepts and eight relations, both fixed.** Adding, removing or changing one is an ontology change.
- **A concept needs its own identity.** Something is a canonical concept only if it has its own identity and other things refer to it.
- **State is derived, never stored.** Commitment state, waits, progress, attention and budgets are pure functions of the records.
- **Core operations are speech acts.** Request, offer, promise, decline, counter-offer, declare done, accept, reject the result, report, ask and announce.
- **Structure is required of agents and suggested to people.** Agents use typed operations directly. People write freely, and the system suggests the matching operation for them to confirm.

## Concepts

### Work

The identity of a lasting piece of work, and nothing more. Its name, status, owner, priority and dates are records, relations or projections attached to it. A Work's identity remains after it closes.

### Party

An identity that writes records and takes roles: a person, an agent, a system, a machine or an organization. These are not separate kinds in the model. Capability, authority and accountability are never derived from the kind. Parties come from [Access](/docs/access); Work does not create a second identity.

### Aim

The declared criterion for when something counts as done. A truth-based Aim is judged by evidence. A judgment-based Aim is judged by a named Party's Declaration.

### Commitment

An obligation an obligor takes on toward an obligee, on behalf of a principal, under Conditions, about an Aim or an Act. It is the unit of work between parties and is always two-sided. Its state is derived from Declarations. Who will accept the result is a required field.

### Condition

A satisfiable predicate that holds progress back. An epistemic Condition is satisfied by Claims. A **Gate** is a normative Condition, satisfied by an eligible Party's Declaration. Authority gates are approved in Access; coordination gates in Work. Dependencies, budgets and SLAs are all Conditions.

### Reference

The identity of something outside Work: a document, a repository, a device, an agent session. Work does not own what a Reference points to.

### Act

An attributable report, written by the Party that did it, of an action taken in the world. An Act is not absolute proof that it happened.

### Declaration

The **only** record type that changes normative state: promises, declines, acceptances, decisions, approvals, assignments, cancellations and amendments. A Declaration is never retroactive.

### Claim

A proposition a Party asserts on some basis. A Claim is not truth and has no normative effect. Its subtypes are Observation, Assessment and Assumption.

## Relations

| Relation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `engaged-in` | A Party's participation in a Work, carrying a role but no obligation |
| `concerns` | An object or record falling within a Work's scope |
| `part-of` | Work composition. Status, owner and visibility are not inherited |
| `serves` | One Aim contributing to another |
| `supersedes` | A revision or replacement. The old record stays in place |
| `derived-from` | Basis, source or lineage, with a typed role: `basis`, `source`, `fulfills` or `supports` |
| `on-behalf-of` | Representation of a principal. It must match the capacity authorized in Access |
| `references` | A link to an external identity |

Dependency is not a relation. "X is waiting on Y" means X has a Condition addressed to Y.

## Roles

The performer, obligor, obligee, principal, acceptor, queue owner and accountable party are separate roles. They live on Commitments and relations, not on the Party. Often one person holds several of them.

## Other terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Queue and pool | Work that has no obligor yet. Every queue has a declared owner |
| Practice | An optional domain template: vocabulary, subtypes and role labels. It can only constrain, never extend the core |
| Ledger | The trust and placement boundary where records are kept. Every record is in exactly one ledger |

## Surfaces

| Surface | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Needs Attention | What do I actually need to do, why now, and what happens if I wait? |
| Briefing | What meaningfully changed while I was away? |
| My Work | Where do the things I take part in stand? |
| Commitments | Who promised me what, and what do I owe to whom? |
| Work View | Where does this work stand, in human-readable form? |
| Live | What is happening in this work right now? |

Chat is a channel attached to work, not a surface. Something binding said in chat binds only once it becomes a typed record.
