## The model at a glance

Access's semantics rest on seven authority primitives, one policy construct, three record types and the values they carry. Records are canonical. The current authority graph is a deterministic projection of those records: a primitive's content lives in the records that create and change it, and its state is derived.

The central idea is a single chain:

- A **Party** holds authority only through a **Grant**, rooted at an **AuthorityAnchor**.
- One of the Party's **Instances** can use part of that authority only within a **Mandate**.
- Each use is an **Authority Exercise**, which Access answers with a **Decision** and a record.

## Actors

### Party

A person or organization with rights and responsibilities: a human, a company, an agent or a service. Access refers to a Party through a product-neutral reference (PartyRef). It does not create Parties or keep a second Party registry.

### Instance

The concrete acting occurrence of a Party, such as a session on a given device or a running agent process. An Instance is the actor of an Exercise and the target of a Mandate. Key rotation keeps the same Instance. A break in continuity creates a new one, and recovery produces a single successor that inherits no authority.

### ActorContext

Who is acting in a given request: either `Authenticated(Instance)` or `Anonymous`. An anonymous actor holds nothing and can use only public grants.

## Authority primitives

### AuthorityDomain

The scope in which authority records are kept, with a single source of truth, usually an organization. Within a domain there is exactly one authoritative lineage for each authority object.

### AuthorityAnchor and root

The point where authority over a resource begins, and that authority's ultimate holder. The root is a value of the anchor: a sole Party, or a joint group of Parties. A resource not covered by an anchor carries no authority.

### Grant

An explicit conferral of authority, and the **only** source of authority. Delegation is also a Grant. Each Grant has exactly one basis, and its terms can only be narrower than that basis. A rule-shaped Grant selects its holders from accepted claims, so the same Grant can select different holders as claims change.

### Mandate

The subset of a Party's authority that one specific Instance may use. Holding an authority does not mean being able to use it everywhere. A Mandate creates no holding, cannot be delegated and is bound to exactly one Instance.

### Acceptance

An explicit, scoped, revocable decision about whose claims are trusted, and for what use. It is the only meaning of "trust" in Access.

### Authority Exercise

The use of an authority for a specific action at a specific moment, together with its decision and record. It is Access's unit of value. Changing authority, such as granting, amending or revoking, is itself an Exercise.

### RestrictionPolicy

A rule that grants nothing. It can only forbid, add a requirement or narrow, for example "no payments on weekends". It is a policy construct, not a source of positive authority.

## Records

All three record types are immutable, attributable and append-only. Corrections are made with a new record.

### Genesis

The single unconditioned record that founds an AuthorityDomain.

### Exercise record

The opening record bound to an intent's nonce, together with its decision records.

### Claim

Someone's assertion about something. A Claim is not the truth itself, but a record of who said what. A signature proves authorship, not accuracy.

## Decisions

### Decision

Access's verdict on an Exercise:

| Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `ALLOW` | The action may proceed, within its intent envelope |
| `DENY` | The action may not proceed |
| `REQUIRE_ACTION` | The action may proceed once what is missing is provided, for example a specific approval |

### Intent and IntentEnvelope

The exact description of the requested action: what, on which resource, with which parameters and for which purpose. An ALLOW is valid only for its envelope.

### Capacity

Every Exercise declares whether the actor acts on its own behalf (`OWN`) or on behalf of another Party (`FOR`). On-behalf-of is derived only from this capacity, never from the lineage.

## Values and boundaries

### ValidityContract

How long and under which conditions a decision or artifact remains valid, and how quickly revocation takes effect. It also covers identity-plane tokens.

### Projection

A short-lived, holder-bound artifact that carries authority outward, such as a token, an SSH certificate or a signed decision receipt. It is evidence of authority, not authority itself.

### Consequence Tier

The risk class of an action, from reads (CT0) to irreversible and critical actions (CT3). Required assurance rises with the tier.

### Role

A named, versioned set of authorities, such as `admin` or `editor`. Assigning a role is a Grant that pins a version. Changing a role definition cannot silently change existing Grants.

## Identity plane

The identity plane is a complete IdP. Its objects are identity-plane objects, not authority primitives. They reach the authority plane only as Claims.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Tenant | The commercial customer |
| Identity Realm | An isolated identity space where a customer's users live. Credentials and login identifiers are local to a realm |
| User record | An account in a realm |
| Credential | A password, passkey, TOTP secret or certificate |
| Session | A login session. It is bound to an Instance, but it is not an Instance |
| Application | An OAuth/OIDC client |
| External IdP | A connected upstream identity provider |
